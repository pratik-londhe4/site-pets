Little pixel pets that walk around your pages, chase a ball and nap on your headings. Plus a desk buddy in wp-admin and a Pet Playground block.

== Description ==

Give your site a pet. A small pixel cat (or dog, fox, rubber duck or Wapuu) walks around your pages, sits down, falls asleep, jumps up onto your headings and images, and runs after a ball when a visitor throws one. Click a pet to pet it.

**Pets on your pages**

* Seven pets: orange, grey and black cats, a dog, a fox, a rubber duck and Wapuu. Up to three at once, each with a name.
* They walk on your page's own layout: the tops of headings, images, quotes and buttons are platforms they jump onto, and they fall off when you scroll them away.
* Visitors can **throw a ball** and watch the pets fetch it, **pet** them (a heart pops up), or **hide** them. A hidden pet stays hidden for that visitor, remembered in their own browser.
* Show them on every page, only on the front page, or only on chosen post types, and choose whether they appear on phones.

**A desk buddy in wp-admin**

Each user can pick a pet on their profile. It walks along the bottom of the admin screens while they work, and only they see it.

**The Pet Playground block**

A little scene (garden, beach or night) with pets playing in it, anywhere in a post. Pick up to three pets, the scene and the height. The editor shows the real pets playing.

**Kind to your site and your visitors**

* No layout shift and no blocked clicks: the pets float above the page and only the pets themselves can be clicked.
* About 8 KB of JavaScript once compressed (25 KB before), plus WordPress's own small hooks library. The pets stop when the tab is in the background.
* People who ask their device for **reduced motion** get pets that sit still.
* Nothing loads from other sites, nothing is tracked, no personal data is stored.

**For developers**

Add your own pets with the `site_pets_types` filter (a sprite sheet of sixteen 32×32 frames), change where they show with `site_pets_show`, and steer them in JavaScript with `wp.hooks` (`sitePets.nextState`, `sitePets.platformSelector`, `sitePets.petted`, ...). WP-CLI (`wp site-pets`) and Abilities API abilities (WordPress 6.9+) are included. See [the reference](https://github.com/pratik-londhe4/site-pets/blob/main/docs/REFERENCE.md).

Free and open source under the GPL. No pro version, no upsells, no ads in wp-admin, no tracking.

== Installation ==

1. Install from Plugins > Add New Plugin, or upload the zip.
2. Activate it. An orange cat named Mango appears on your pages straight away.
3. Choose your pets under **Appearance > Site Pets**. Pick a desk buddy under **Users > Profile**.

== Frequently Asked Questions ==

= Can visitors turn the pets off? =

Yes. The **Hide pets** button hides them for that visitor, and they stay hidden on later visits (the choice is kept in the visitor's own browser). You can turn the button off, and turn the pets off for phones.

= Do the pets slow my site down? =

The pets script and engine are about 8 KB compressed, each pet's sprite sheet is under 2 KB, and nothing runs while the tab is in the background. The pets layer floats above the page, so it causes no layout shift.

= The pets never climb onto my content. =

They jump onto the tops of headings, images, figures, quotes, tables, buttons and separators that are on screen and within reach of the bottom of the window. On a tall screen most of the page is too high; they will still walk, sit and play along the bottom. Developers can change which elements count with the `sitePets.platformSelector` JavaScript filter.

= Can I add my own pet? =

Yes, with a sprite sheet in the same layout as the plugin's: one row of sixteen 32×32 frames (idle ×2, walk ×4, run ×4, sit ×2, sleep ×2, jump, fall), facing right. Register it with the `site_pets_types` filter. The plugin's sheets are generated by `tools/sprites/generate.py` in the GitHub repository, which you can use as a starting point.

= Who made the art? =

The pets are original pixel art drawn for this plugin, released under the GPL like the code. Wapuu is the WordPress mascot, created by Kazuko Kaneuchi and released under the GPL; this is a new pixel drawing of the character.
